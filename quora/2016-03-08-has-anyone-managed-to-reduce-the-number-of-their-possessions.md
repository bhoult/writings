# Has anyone managed to reduce the number of their possessions to 100?

I have been working on a minimalist lifestyle for over 10 years and I am nowhere near less than 100 possessions. Depending on how you count them I have more than that in just computer parts.

When I was a kid I was living with a small tribe in Kenya (Pokot). I had a couple Kenyan friends that I used to hang out with. I remember visiting their home and at a glance I could see everything they owned. It consisted of the following:

A hut made from sticks, mud and straw that they had made themselves.
A bed that they made themselves out of sticks.
A aluminum cooking pot that they shared.
A school uniform (shirt and shorts)
A notebook and pencil for school.
A pair of shoes made from old car tires.
The clothes they were wearing.
A sharpened piece of metal that they used as a knife.
A panga (machete) for gardening.
A plastic cup.
A goat.
Two chickens.
A homemade bow with two arrows.

They were not attempting to become minimalists, they would have had more if they could. But like any first world person that visits a third world country, I was shocked at how little they had, but even more so how happy and generous they were in spite of that. I am not saying their lives were idyllic, they were often hungry and they were desperate for any opportunity to improve their situation. They were innocent of consumerism though, until we introduced them to it.

When I returned to the United States for college, the contrast in how people lived made it seems like they were dragons hovering over a mound of gold that they had no use for other than denying it to others. Like dragons they lived by consuming the lives and prosperity of the little people. I remember going to a church that had a $8000 flower arrangement that they purchased each week. For the first time I was ashamed to be an American. I could not fathom how with the best of intentions we had come to this.

Our possessions shelter us from a reality we try desperately not to see or acknowledge our participation in. The reality is that living with less than 100 things is only a challenge to those for whom it matters little. For the majority of the world it is the default.

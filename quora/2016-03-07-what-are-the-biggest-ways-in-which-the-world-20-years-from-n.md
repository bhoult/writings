# What are the biggest ways in which the world 20 years from now will probably be different from today? What are the biggest "X factors" (changes that are not probable, but are possible and could be huge)?

There are many answers here that I would agree with. Some of the most obvious are the following: 

Drone tech leading to flying vehicles. Probably these will be mostly autonomous so that they will not require a great deal of training.

Self driving cars and trucks becoming common and disrupting employment among truck drivers which are the bulk of the current "middle class". This will also lead to greater efficiency with personal delivery which will further disrupt local stores and those they employ.

Advances in solar and battery storage will make electric vehicles more practical. It seems like the Arab states are dumping oil at below cost to disrupt competition from shale and sand oil from becoming established. This can only be sustained for a limited period after which alternative energy will become more of a priority.

A growing world population will continue to pollute air and water unsustainably. This will lead to increased tension between the rich who can ship their waste elsewhere, and poor who will continue to deal with the consequences of the actions of others.

I also foresee a new industry forming around automation from small scale electronics like the arduino, raspberry pi and other SBC's being combined with artificial intelligence, 3d printing and crowdsourcing. This will enable small scale automation of tasks by hobbyists that do not have the mass market appeal necessary to get the traditional funding for mass production. This will be most evident in agriculture and small scale farming.

The global access to information provided by the internet combined with global competition and the availability of specialty components available from the likes of amazon has accelerated the rate of change in our society beyond what evolution has made people comfortable with. This has resulted in generational disparity as older generations are left behind by technology they don't understand while they still control the bulk of the physical assets and political power. 

On the negative side, the internet has provided confirmation and community validating every contradictory view. Combine this with the increasing polarization of political, economic, religious and social views along with the marginalization of experts in favor of entertainers and it seems inevitable that a mass breakdown will eventually result. Further complicating this is the increased sophistication of interconnections between critical components of infrastructure such that the whole becomes larger and more brittle over time. This tends to result in the potential of large systems failing in ways that effect a larger number of people in more critical ways for longer periods of time. 

I fear that polarization will combine with the arrogance supplied by quasi-scientific internet echo-chambers to produce an increasingly young, unemployed, frustrated and powerless lower class. They will be angered by the obfuscation and misunderstanding of older generations who may be out of touch with the speed of change and yet control most of the money and power. They will be incited to attack the increasingly complex infrastructure which upon failure will result in even more anger. Scapegoats will be found, people will behave badly. The downward spiral has already begun.

If we can continue to provide the technical trinkets and entertainment central to consumerism long enough to close the economic gap and enter into a post-scarcity society then we may avert calamity. Without the belief that people have a fair and equitable chance to live their lives the polarization of other aspects will never be addressed and the system will eventually fail.

When it fails hopefully the generation that follows will not be so short sited and arrogant as we who put them there.

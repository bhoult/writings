# How much does pack weight affect hiking speed?

Its importance is proportional to the distance you plan to travel. If a couple miles then it does not matter much so long as you can lift it. On longer hikes it becomes probably the most important factor apart from having the essentials (water, food) when you need them.

Hiking hundreds of miles with a heavy pack will make you slower and more miserable to the point that you will be tempted to quit. Also you will risk injury trying to push to keep up with others carrying lighter loads. So get rid of all non essentials, don't duplicate stuff that you can borrow from your hiking partners. Don't carry stuff that would "come in handy". Get the least and lightest gear possible.

Consider the amount of effort it would take to carry a one ton automobile up a one mile hill. Carrying a single pound of extra gear over a 2000 mile trail like the AT would take the same effort. It is the difference between an enjoyable stroll and a painful sore-footed death march.

Also, try to lose as much body weight as possible before you start. It is all the same as far as your feet are concerned.

I speak from experience here. I hiked the AT with an 85lb pack. I was an idiot.

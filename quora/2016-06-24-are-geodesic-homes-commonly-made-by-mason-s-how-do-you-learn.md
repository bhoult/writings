# Are geodesic homes commonly made by Mason's? How do you learn to build one?

Using traditional building materials and techniques geodesic domes are extremely hard to make and maintain. The problem is that traditional buildings rely on right angles and there are none in a geodesic dome. Every joint has to be calculated and if there are any errors it will leak. There is no flat roof to cover the structure so every joint becomes a potential problem. They don't make curved furniture and appliances so nothing will fit correctly inside. Pretty much the only way is to get a prefab kit that is computer designed and has all the pieces pre-cut. These are not common so they will be more expensive. You will still have maintenance issues long term but it may be worth it to you.

That being said, I have been living in a non-traditional geodesic dome for the last five years and love it. Technically it is the strongest structure you can build with the least materials and they can be made to cover huge opens paces without internal supports. I built mine myself out of papercrete and conduit pipe in about two months for about $3000. It is pretty much maintenance free and easy to fix if it ever has a problem. Check my blog if you want details. Mine is only 19ft in diameter. Too small for most people, but there are other options. Check out earth bag domes or monolithic domes.

Made non-traditionally they are very efficient, inexpensive and practical. But you won't find any construction companies willing to build one, and it will not meet zoning requirements or building codes so will have to be built outside city limits.

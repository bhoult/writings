# Is learning HTML and CSS as your first language a good start?

A language is a means to communicate an idea. In programming you are communicating with an extremely pedantic idiot with perfect memory. To communicate you describe ideas as objects (nouns), you describe their characteristics with adjectives, what they do with verbs and how they do it with adverbs.

Html is analogous with the nouns. You describe the things that are there, the content.

CSS is the adjectives, it describes the characteristics of the nouns, how they look.

If you stop here you can describe a static scene, like a photograph or a painting… or a webpage. But your language is incomplete without verbs and adverbs.

For those you need a (programming) aspect to the language in addition to the html and CSS (markup). Programming provides the verbs and adverbs of a language, it says what things do and how it is done. On the browser at the moment that is JavaScript, but it could be any of hundreds of languages on the server.

JavaScript manipulates things (html) that look a certain way (CSS), directly on the browser. The result is markup in motion that responds. Like a series of still pictures gives a movie the illusion of motion, or how reacting to stimulus give a robot the appearance of life.

Server side languages (C, Python, ruby, node, PHP, Perl, .Net, etc.) do the same except it is more like a remote control where the browser is what is being controlled remotely. By sending a sequence of stills (html and CSS), and recieving form data, it creates the illusion of life with which a person will interact.

# I am a terrible programmer. I understand things well, but I’m terrible at implementing them. What should I do to improve?

Imagine someone saying "I am terrible at hammering, what should I do to improve?" Probably the first thing you would say is "What are you hammering on?" Because nobody just goes and starts beating on something without a purpose. The hammer is just a means to an end. If the end is not important to you then the means to achieve it will be similarly unimportant.

Likewise with programming, what do you hope to accomplish that motivates you to continue to learn. What keeps you looking and trying to solve a problem for days or months? If the end is just to pass a class or some other motivation that is ultimately unimportant to you then the means to achieve it will be similarly unimportant.

I would suggest finding a problem that you are passionate about, something that others are also passionate about. Volunteer to work on it and get the other people to hold you accountable. It would be nice if the other people are programmers, but not necessary. Just having others interested in what you are doing is a great motivator.

For example, if homelessness is important to you then perhaps write an application that would help organize shelters, or food programs. There are so many real world problems that need assistance. Then programming will have purpose instead of just writing the billionth version of hello world. Then you will have reason to work on it, then you will become a better programmer.

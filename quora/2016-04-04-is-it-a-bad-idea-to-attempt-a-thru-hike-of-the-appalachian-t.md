# Is it a bad idea to attempt a thru-hike of the Appalachian Trail if you're out-of-shape?

I trained for five years, carrying a 100lb pack up a 1000ft hill and back three days a week. Over 3000 miles in training. 

It helped. I finished, and never had any serious issues or injuries. I started out carrying an 85lb pack which I did not think would be a problem since I had trained with more. It was a problem.... although I could walk, it was unnecessarily slow and painful. I would have changed to ultralight except that I had already blown my budget on gear "that would last".

It is now 12 years later, and I am definitely out of shape again. I did not plan it this way but with a desk job and long commute it did not take long. However I would not hesitate to attempt another long distance hike in my current condition because I learned one simple truth. The body helps, but in the end it is all a mind game.

The secret is pacing yourself and hiking smart. If you lose as much body weight as you can and get the lightest pack and gear (10-30lbs total) it will go a long way. If you learn to pace yourself and stick to it you will get in shape as you go. 

This is not as easy as it sounds. Many of the people you meet at the beginning will be in excellent shape and you will want to keep up with them. If you can't you will become discouraged, if you try you will injure yourself and will be in too much pain to enjoy the hike.

If you are not in shape you should start early and resign yourself to doing small mileage for the first couple months. Like 3-5 miles a day or even less. Find your optimum breathing rate and try to maintain that constantly. You should be breathing hard but not struggling all the time. It should be a rate you can maintain for hours at a time.

If you are going up hill try to keep your pace the same just reduce the size of your steps. You may just be taking little one inch steps all the way up. But your heart rate and breathing should stay the same. 

Take breaks whenever you need to. This might be a lot initially. You will feel like you are never going to make it. You will be constantly sore and hungry. You will see others speeding past you constantly never to be seen again. You will get discouraged and want to quit.

The mind game.

Don't give up. If you pay attention you will notice that very gradually you are becoming stronger. You will be losing weight, which makes each hill easier. By a few months in you will be part of a group that goes about your pace. 

Continue to be mindful of injury and don't over do it even when you think you are in good shape. You muscles and cardiovascular condition will improve faster than your ligaments and joints. Tearing muscles, sprained ankles and joint pain can end your hike.

If finishing is your goal then the final mind game comes into play. 70-80% quit before they finish. For about half it is some injury when they were too exhausted and were not being careful. For the rest it was mental. About 1/3 of the way through the novelty has worn off. If you are not enjoying the hike for what it is and are thinking of it as just something to achieve then you will start to wonder if it is worth it. It will become just another job. I could always tell when someone was talking themselves into quitting. They would start talking about their friends at home, their girlfriend / boyfriend. The comforts they were missing. Their old life. A few weeks later, they were gone.

People on the AT take new "Trail Names" for the duration. You have to leave your old life completely and engage fully in what you are doing now. Don't let yourself start comparing what you are doing with what you could be doing. Don't let the idea that you could quit any time start to fester and take root.

One final note. Don't expect anyone to be impressed if you finish the trail. They will not even be able to comprehend it, and will neither understand why you did it, or what you learned from it. That being said, if you do it for your own sake it may be the most significant experience of your life. You will never be the same person again.

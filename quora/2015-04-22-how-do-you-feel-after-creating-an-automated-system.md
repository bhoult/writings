# How do you feel after creating an automated system?

I was part of a small team that built a system about 10 years or so ago which I believe was the first automated end to end price estimation program for the intermodal shipping industry. Prior to this the industry wide norm was to have experts in various regions. Each expert maintained stacks of printed spreadsheets in which a carrier basically says they will take a specific size load from a to b for a given price. It is still hard for me to believe they did it this way, but essentially if you wanted to ship from one location to another it typically involved loading on a truck which went to a rail yard then to another truck which delivered to the destination. So the experts on trucks at each end would get together with the appropriate rail expert and they would flip through their ratesheets to try to find the best end to end prices. Sometimes they had to figure out thousands of these.

I worked for one of the smaller intermodal logistics companies and over a few years we were able to upload all the various formats of rate sheets, and calculate all the various options in seconds instead of the days it took previously. It felt awesome. The users loved it and they were able to handle many times the load and go home early. 

This is why I got into programming in the first place. I hate repetitive mindless tasks, and I thought that finally something I had done was saving about 50 people from having to do such menial work for the rest of their careers.

After a few years of development we reached the point where we could do live online quotes. We built a public front end and batch processing capabilities. Our ruby on rails app could provide quotes in just a few seconds instead of days. We were pretty excited to see how it would be received in the industry. 

A couple of weeks later the entire company was bought by a much larger intermodal company. Apparently the owners were asked how much they wanted, they gave an absurd amount because they did not want to sell and the bigger company said ok. They shut down our program immediately and only used it internally while they developed their own version in .net. A year later they had laid off all the original 50 or so employees after transitioning their existing accounts. After a couple years the last remaining employee from the old company said that they had developed their version to the point that it was being used internally but it had a lot of issues and took several minutes to return results. He was still using the version we developed but since it had not been maintained for a couple years it was becoming less useful.

Needless to say, I did not feel that good about it in the end. The realities of business turned something I thought would help a lot of people into something that probably got them all fired and made a few of the owners rich. Meanwhile I made a modest salary for the few years we worked on it. Turns out that if you make something too disruptive to established players they will kill and bury it no matter how useful... perhaps because it was too useful.

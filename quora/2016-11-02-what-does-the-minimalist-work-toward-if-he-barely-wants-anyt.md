# What does the minimalist work toward if he barely wants anything?

What do you hope to accomplish with the thing that you want?

I believe most people tend to see products as pre-packaged solutions to problems. In some cases that may be true, but the majority of the time you are trading one set of problems for another.

For example, if the problem is you need to get to work you might want to buy a car. This trades the problem of getting to work for the problem of having to make car payments, deal with insurance, buy fuel, tires, repairs. Most of the time people are aware of this on some level, so they tend to try to justify the trade with the idea that it is an “investment” that will empower them. So it has to be newish in order to “be more reliable”. And it has to look good, “So it will be presentable for work and stuff”. And then they downplay the cost with mind games, so it is not $30,000, it is just “$500/mo”.

Minimalism in part is about seeing “things” for what they are. If you just need transportation, you may be able to get an older car, or a bicycle, or ride-share. If what you really want is to buy into the narrative of the car commercial, or the image of success that society expects, then understand that perhaps your self image is the problem instead of transportation.

So, what is it that you are working toward? Is it the solution to a real problem or do you just desire a product? Will you have to make the “problem” into something greater than it actually is to later justify the expense? Why can’t you do what you want? Is there a car / house /credit card payment chaining you to a job that you hate? How did that happen? Was it really inevitable, just the way life is?

A minimalist wants many things, like perhaps community, maybe family, possibly solutions to a broken world. In every pursuit there are “problems” which prevent progress. In most cases “products” are placebos that fail in the long term. In many cases “products” are themselves the problem.

In the time you have left I hope you find a problem worthy of your consideration.

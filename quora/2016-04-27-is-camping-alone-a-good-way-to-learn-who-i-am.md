# Is camping alone a good way to learn who I am?

Camping alone can be a good means of self discovery. It accomplishes two things: (camping) which gets you out of your normal life where you are conditioned to have automatic responses to familiar stimulus. (Alone) where you are not distracted by outside influences. 

As others have mentioned you will likely be bored for a while. This is part of the process, it takes time for you to quit the pattern of finding familiar distractions. You have to get past this, for the first several days you will experience a desire to do something that distracts you like watching tv, followed by the realization that you can't, followed by the disappointment and thinking "this sucks, I am so bored, I should quit". If you do quit then you will just remember it as a failed bad experience. You have to get to the point of accepting your current reality. When you are no longer thinking about what you could be doing you will begin to think about what you are doing now. For me this takes about three days. But you have to avoid distractions like books, electronics, music etc. I tend to try to hike into somewhere for three days, then camp when I am past the self pitying stage.

I have found fasting to help as well, for some reason not eating for a few days reduces mental background noise and allows extended periods of clarity and concentration. For the first couple days I just think about food.... A lot. But on the third day (for me) the hunger goes away and I can really start to focus. Exercise during this time will be exhausting, so I usually just find a place to camp and stay for a while. Generally a week or two. As long as you don't smell food you will not get hungry and your mind will be in that state that you sometimes find right before you go to sleep or before you get out of bed when you wake up. The epiphany zone. Drink lots of liquid though, I usually make unsweetened black herbal teas without caffeine. As a bonus you will loose about a pound and a half per day. I also usually bring a book when fasting. Something philosophical that will inspire thought. Most of my experiences with this have been life-changing in minor ways.

Another route that may be easier would be a silent retreat center. You can find these in almost any state, most are open to any religion or lack thereof. There are other people but there is a rule that nobody is allowed to talk outside of specific times or places. They usually provide food and a place to sleep as well as counselors.

Be aware that with any of this a lot of negative emotions are likely to come out, stuff that we have learned to avoid by reflexively finding distraction. It is not uncommon for people trying this for the first time to spend hours weeping without really knowing why. Again , this is something you have to get past.

Good luck, I hope you find more of yourself on the other side.

# What was something you had to get used to once returning to your life from the Appalachian Trail?

I went back to work. It was tedious repetitive and stressful. I sat in a office and was more exhausted than if I had spent the whole day walking. The view never changed. I could not look back from the mountaintop and see where I had been. I could not see where I was going. I did not know what was to be achieved by going there. But I went through the motions anyway.

My community was gone. The people I met now were no longer part of our common journey. We had nothing to talk about. Maybe meeting some deadline, ideas to make a process more efficient. We did not care about the same thing any longer. A linear path that makes all people equal. Where once we knew and shared each others goals, ambitions, hopes. Now it was just coming together to do what needed to be done so we could rush to go apart. There was no shared hope in a destination so there was no joy in the journey.

It was clear that something was wrong. This was “real life”, how could bugs, sweat, hunger and pain be so much more satisfying. But the final discovery was that nobody who had not experienced anything different could see the problem, or could value a solution, or even conceive of making the attempt. My descriptions were inadequate, just more incoherent noise. Without the experience we were foreigners to each other. I would dream about how it had been in our simplified long narrow world, how it could be, they would ask where we should go when lunchtime comes around and remark on some character in some TV show.

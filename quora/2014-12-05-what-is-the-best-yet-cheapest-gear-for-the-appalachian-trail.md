# What is the best, yet cheapest gear for the Appalachian Trail?

On a long distance hike like this the "best" gear is the least gear. Consider how hard it would be to carry a one ton automobile about a 1000 ft. up and down a one mile hill. Then consider that the AT is about 2174 miles so for every pound you add it would take the equivalent effort of carrying that automobile. When I did it I started with about 85lbs... I was an idiot.

So with that in mind carry as few things as possible and the lightest things you can get. Also try to recognize the mindset of having some product to deal with every hypothetical situation. The reality on the AT is that you will encounter other people all the time and you will go through towns that you can get stuff if it turns out you really need it. If you happen to need a bit of duct tape, just ask and someone else will probably be happy to share some with you. 

Also consider that you can probably stay in one of the shelters... sometimes they fill up which is inconvenient, but you could probably do the whole thing without a tent. I used a hammock, but sent it home for the last third... so maybe just get a sil-nylon tarp instead. 

So to answer your specific questions.
1. Small umbrella works better (for me) than most rain gear. I sweat enough while hiking in raingear that I might as well just get wet. Maybe get some frog-togs for when it is cold and rainy.

2. Insulating clothing - use layering as much as possible, if it is cold and wet then have some kind of cheap waterproof shell, if just cold then layer multiple fleece or wool shirts. If hot then just get wet. You can get all this stuff at a second-hand store.

3. Tent - Do without... get a sheet of tyvec or make a tarp. Plan to stay in shelters for the most part.

4. Stoves - Either make a alcohol stove from a soda can, or just make a small fire with three rocks. Plan on eating mostly things that don't need to be cooked. You will hit a town at least once a week where you can pig out, when hiking food is just to keep you going.

5. Etc. There are "hiker boxes" in shelters, stores, hostels where people put extra food and gear. I know one guy who started the trail with no money and just what he was wearing. He basically got everything from these boxes. Also if you start on the approach trail around March 15 people discard a ton of gear on that first section. The year I did it, one lady left everything on the side of the trail with a note saying anyone could take what they wanted, the trail was too hard and she was going home. But apart from that there were spare sleeping pads, knives, tents, cooking gear, food, just about everything you can imagine.

Another hiker calculated that the best calorie to cost ratio was hotdogs and beer. His strategy was to go to a town, buy a large amount of the cheapest hotdogs and beer he could find. Then go out into the woods, get drunk, eat cold hotdogs until he was full, get more drunk until he passed out for a couple days. He claimed that he felt rested and ready to go after a couple days of this.... I would not suggest it though.

What money you have should probably be spent on shoes and a couple pair of socks (I suggest bridgedale socks and some dress socks to use as sock liners). On shoes get the stiffest soles you can find ... they should be really hard to bend with your hands and make sure you break them in. Get a stick or a piece of bamboo for a hiking staff. If you keep your pack weight under 25lbs then you won't need a fancy pack with internal support and all that. Also a nice sleeping bag would be worth spending money on. You can make do with military surplus or something like that, but a good down bag will be lighter and warmer and sleep is pretty important. Also, get one of those cheap foam pads. Also consider aquamira for water treatment or if you are really cheap a couple drops of bleach.

There was one girl in 2004 that completed the trail carrying only 8lbs. She had basically a waterproof poncho and a wool blanket with some stuff wrapped up in that she folded around a camera strap. The poncho was her tent, raingear and what she wore while she washed her clothes. She only ate trailmix and sometimes drank straight olive oil if she needed the energy before a big hill. I felt like a pansy after I met her, she left me in the dust.

Good luck on your hike.

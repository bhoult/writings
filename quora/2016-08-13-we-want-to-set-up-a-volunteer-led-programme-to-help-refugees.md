# We want to set up a volunteer led programme to help refugees learn coding and find work, what programming would you suggest starting with?

Having used over a dozen languages, I often wonder which are the most teachable. In the end learnability tends to be determined by the interests of the ones learning. The smaller the loop between effort and reward the more likely they are to percieve benefit and the more apt they will be to learn.

So the choice of language would be determined by which would be most tangably useful in their situation. Among refuges I would like to interview them to learn what they most hope to accomplish with programming.

I expect finding a job would be obvious but a little abstract. They would be having to just trust that it will eventually lead to a better future even if they have no knowledge or interest in the actual work or a clear view in how it would benefit them other than a paycheck. The loop is large, they are solving their employers problem who is solving their clients problem, who is solving their customers problem. Then they get a paycheck which they hope will mitigate their immediate problem so they can persue something they really care about.

What you want is something they can use to simultaneously mitigate their income problem while empowering to do what they care about immediately.

For ease of learning I would probably go with python. But for universalty and availability javascript may be more practical. I would think having gone through a major injustice that a tool that would help them disseminate relevant information would be a way to fight back.

Javascript being the language of the Web might be a more immediately beneficial tool. Of course the added complication of the whole asynchronous mindset may offset that benefit to some degree.

Overall I suggest you spend more effort determining what problem they want to solve before you recommend a tool to address it.

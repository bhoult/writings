# Why am I feeling that the ancient, natural life was better than the present, artificial life? Is it true?

At some point in your life you start to question what your efforts have accomplished. Most of what we do in modern society is comparatively intangible. We have jobs where we are paid to solve problems that other people have. The problems are abstract, we flip a burger because someone else is hungry, we make a website so someone we don’t know can sell a product we don’t need to someone we will never meet, hopefully for more than they can afford. We study things we will probably never use because someone else told us to do so, and we paid them for telling us this.

After enough years have passed we look back and see the end result of our efforts are as ephemeral as the dust. A more sophisticated version of the dog chasing its tail. We realize that our life has been poured into futility that will be forgotten shortly after we are gone and likely ignored until then.

And then we do something real.

We build a cabin from trees we chopped down and it provides shelter. We grow a garden, harvest and eat the food and it provides nourishment. We watch the sun set and feel the breeze coming in off the lake in the evening while we enjoy a cup of tea that we grew ourselves and know satisfaction.

Better is solving your own problems, and receiving the benefits from your own labor. At the end of the day you participated and were part of a life that we have been living for untold years. From the outside you can see the foolishness of this bubble that presumes to dominate rather than participate.

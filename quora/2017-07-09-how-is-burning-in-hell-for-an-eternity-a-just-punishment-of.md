# How is burning in Hell for an eternity a just punishment of committing a finite amount of sin?

John woke up into darkness. In the distance he thought he saw a barely perceptible speck of light. He started to get up to investigate when he heard a voice. “don't go that way, it is a trap.”

John called out “who is that? Where am I? How did I get here?” The voice responded, “I don't know the answers. Like you I woke in this place. But there was another person here who told me to go the other way, into the darkness. He told me to trust him. I followed him far enough to see a figure standing at a door. He said he would come back and get rid of the trap but he needed some supplies. He asked me to stay a while to warn anyone else that came along. And then he was gone.

John asked where this person was, why he left, why he should trust him. The voice had no answers, merely that he chose to trust the voice he heard.

John said “You expect me to believe someone came in here to remove a trap just because he is a nice guy…likely story, there is always an ulterior motive. He is the one leading you into a trap. You are probably working with him. You are a fool to trust a voice in the darkness rather than your own senses.” He begin to go toward the light carefully feeling his way. The voice continued to plead with him, warning that it would lead to his death.

John became angry, the floor seemed smooth so he got up and started rapidly walking toward the light. He yelled “I did not ask to be here, you have no proof, why should I pay any attention to a voice in the darkness? I am getting out of here.”

Suddenly there was a scream followed a few seconds later by a loud thud, and then silence.

A few minutes passed and a new voice said “hey, where am I?”

John would lay dead at the bottom of the cliff for eternity. Did he “deserve” it? Was he being punished by the person in the darkness?

Decisions have consequences. Often they are not fair. Sometimes they are eternal.

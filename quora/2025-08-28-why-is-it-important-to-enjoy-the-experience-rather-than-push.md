# Why is it important to enjoy the experience rather than push for speed when hiking a long trail, and how does this affect your overall journey?

For context I finished the AT in 2004, and did a lot of other trails besides. I am 53 now. When I was young it was all about completing the goal. Enjoyment of the experience was a distant secondary. Each time I finished I was disappointed. It was an adventure, but now it was over. Back to “normal life”.

I think at the time I assumed that I would gain some kind of attribute as a result. Maybe upon hearing of my achievements I would gain some measure of respect or authority. In case you were wondering that never happened. Those that understood were competitive, and those that did not were disinterested. If you have the charisma to accompany the achievement you may have more success in this.

In time I found that one should avoid doing “something” for “something else”, the “something else” is usually just an assumed prize that never existed. On the few occasions when it is grasped I found it lacked any real value. 

But then I found myself reminiscing about the journey. About the joy and satisfaction I had when I looked at and appreciated what was directly in front of me rather than far in the imagined distance. I realized that I had overlooked the important parts. 

 Do “something” because you love it for its own sake.

Now at 53 I still hike, I try to go nowhere for no reason. I tell nobody. Appreciation is a skill I still suck at. I am trying to learn though, because the present is where I will live what remains of my days. Regardless of the lies my ambitions still tell.

# If you were starving and a man with infinite slices of bread only gave you one slice, how would you feel?

I would feel hungry. In fact, still starving.

I am surprised that most of the answers here claim that they would feel grateful. Grateful that someone who could save your life without any cost to himself (infinite bread), decided not to let you die (no bread) and not to let you live (more bread) but instead chose to taunt you with a little more life. Just enough to fully realize that you are going to die next to a mountain of bread because this one man chose to deny it to you.

Let's change the circumstances a bit.

What if it was not only you but your family / friends / children that were starving?

What if you were both starving and found the infinite bread machine together? The other man claimed that he touched it first so it was his and said he would let you have just one piece.

Would it change anything if he himself built the infinite bread machine, or if he inherited the machine, or if he just found the machine?

What if he found the machine and promised he would give you and your family a regular bread allowance if you killed anyone else that tried to use the machine ?

What if instead of a bread machine it is fertile soil, clean water, comfortable shelter? What if the resource was free and infinite but you could only use it at a certain rate without breaking it?

What if there was a distance between the starving man and the bread being left to rot? Not so far as to make it impossible, but enough that it would take a little effort to get the bread to the starving man. Like the distance from your house to the Syrian refugee camps for example. What if the men with the bread were too busy watching TV and could not be bothered to notice the starving man?

What you are describing is the moment before a revolution. When the starving realize that they have nothing to loose by violently attacking whatever stands between them and their salvation. An individual without an army to protect the him would be a fool to get in the way of a starving man while brandishing a single piece of bread.

Civilization has trained us to believe that the possessor of a resource can dictate its use. Starvation would quickly expose the superficiality of that narrative.

When someone gives you something you want but don't need you should feel grateful. When someone capriciously prevents you from getting what you need to survive without reason, the correct response would be rage and the likely outcome would be murder.

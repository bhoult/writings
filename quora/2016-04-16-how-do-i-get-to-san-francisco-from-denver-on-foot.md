# How do I get to San Francisco from Denver on foot?

If you can I would suggest hitchhiking. I know it is considered to be dangerous but I expect that is mostly modern paranoia. Still, don't be afraid to turn down a ride if you feel uncomfortable. If hitchhiking is out then check Craigslist, there are often people that want a traveling companion to go with them and help with driving. If you are not wanting to do either of those then get a cheap bicycle at a yard sale. It will be a lot faster.

The important thing is to do it quickly. The longer it takes the more food you will need. And you will need to eat a lot more if you are walking all day. I hiked the Appalachian trail which is about 2100 miles, but it took me seven months and about $4000 in food. I also had good equipment and trained for 5 years. If you do end up walking try to do most of it at night, it will be cooler, there will be less cars and you will be able to see them coming. Take a flashlight, but leave it off most of the time.

25 miles a day is pretty ambitious unless you are in really good shape. That is more than a full marathon every day, except it will be harder since you are carrying a pack. 10 miles a day would be more realistic. Also bring some bleach and coffee filters to purify water. You will probably have to drink from a lot of creeks. Coffee filters will remove sediment, add a drop of bleach per liter and wait 45 minutes or so. Getting sick will be expensive, and if you can't get to a hospital it could be fatal.

You may consider just getting some work as a day laborer or dishwasher until you can save up for a bus ticket or moped. Just ask every time you pass a store or restaurant. Check local bulletin boards and classified ads, look for help wanted signs. It can't hurt and you will probably need a break from walking anyway.

Probably the most important advice I can give you is to try to think of this as an adventure, or at least a grand challenge. If you manage it it will be an impressive accomplishment. Focus on that.

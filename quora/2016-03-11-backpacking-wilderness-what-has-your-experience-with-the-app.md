# Backpacking (wilderness): What has your experience with the Appalachian Trail been like?

I did a northbound through-hike in 2004. So as not to repeat myself, here is a blog article I wrote about it several years ago: Minimal Intentions 

I still think about it even now (12 years later). I have several friends that I still see pretty regularly that I met on the trail. I believe the AT is probably unique in the social aspect. There are several long distance trails in the world, but none have the community and culture that seems to have become established around the AT. Perhaps because it is the oldest of the long distance trails.

I believe those that complete it are changed. At least that has been my observation of myself and my friends that I know from the hike. We are all slightly weird, just a little different. I think peoples lifestyles have common boundaries which they tend to stay within because outside of those boundaries is unknown. This is reflected in their values and lifestyles. It is much like the trail actually... the volume of people walking wears a path, then people follow the worn path because it is there. Much of the things that people do unquestioning, they do out of fear of what might happen if they stepped off that path. This then becomes what people think of as "normal".

After completing the AT, you realize that a lot of the "normal" is untrue or unnecessary. Most people have never lived for six months for a few thousand dollars and the contents of what they are carrying on their back. If you have this experience then it will set you apart from 99% of your peers. When you understand this, the normal fears; what if I lose my job, my house, my car etc. do not carry the same weight. You can do without. Priorities become different, the things that you do "because I have no choice" start to look less inevitable, as though your range of options have widened. You realize that comfort is optional and sometimes discomfort is preferable. Things that were obstacles become merely challenges. Previous restrictions can be overcome.

But this will make you weirder, you will become strange, your priorities will be different. The things you do and consider will be outside the "normal". It may alienate you if you let it, it may frustrate you that people cannot see things in the ways that seems obvious to you. You will have the option to be changed.

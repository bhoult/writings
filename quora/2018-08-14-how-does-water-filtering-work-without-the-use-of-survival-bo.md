# How does water filtering work without the use of survival bottles but materials found in the wilderness?

Your best bet is to boil it. If you can't do that then look for the cleanest source possible. A natural spring coming out of a rock is the best and is probably safe to drink directly.

If you can't find that then collect water dripping from a rock wall instead of drinking directly from a creek. It will take a while to collect but is less likely to have feces or dead animals in it somewhere upstream.

If you can't do that then dig a hole some distance from the creek and let it fill with water. Throw the water out until it becomes clear. The earth will filter the water between the creek and the hole. Try to dig in sand.

If you have time you can make a filter by filling a piece of bamboo or a hollow log or rock with gravel, charcoal and sand starting from the bottom. The gravel will keep the charcoal from falling out, the charcoal (from a hardwood fire) will remove toxic minerals and will kill some bacteria and viruses and the sand on top will remove sediment.

If you have anything made of silver, let it sit in your water container. Silver kills bacteria and viruses but will take a long time to do so if it is like a silver dime or jewelry, since the surface area is small. Pioneers used to put a silver dollar in their water barrels for this purpose.

If you have a clear container, or open bowl then let the water sit in direct sunlight for as long as possible. Ultraviolet light will kill some bacteria and viruses.

If you have bleach a couple drops per litre will purify water but you have to let it sit for about an hour.

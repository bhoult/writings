# How does traveling as a child shape your perspective on what really matters in life compared to material possessions?

I expect we can never really know. We can only return to the road we did not travel in our imagination. There is probably a study that might tell us statistically but I am not aware of it.

I was from the US, but spent my high school years in rural Africa (Kenya). I know I came back weird. We had church sitting on a log under a tree for shade. Old women covered in flies would sit next to me, but we talked about hope and redemption and community. The first church I attended on my return bragged how they spent over $10,000 on the flower arrangements for their televised services.

It broke me, something in my head could never heal from that. Now 35 years later I am 53 years old. I live playing a part in an imaginary land where constructs made of words obscure a reality that we collectively agree not to look beyond. We’ve built a whole network of theologies to make us the heroes of our own stories.

I am not any better. I also play the game and avoid looking at what I once saw clearly. I devote most of my days to solving imaginary problems that we made ourselves.

Travel may do that to you, it may widen the view of your world before you learn to avert your gaze.

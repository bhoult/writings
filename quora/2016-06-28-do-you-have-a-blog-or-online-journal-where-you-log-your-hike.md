# Do you have a blog or online journal where you log your hikes and backpacking adventures?

Looking at your bio it looks like we must have been on the AT at the same time… I also did a through hike northbound in 2004. My trail name was “spin cycle”.

I do a lot of reading… probably a book a week on average. I prepared for the AT for five years so read just about everything I could find at the time. Which amounted to a couple good books, a bunch of really poorly written ones and a few online “trail journals” that I could find. These days, there are probably thousands of these journals… in 2004 there were only a few.

The common thread in most of these books and journals was that reading about a hike is not really that entertaining. Reading a story in which the context is a hike is better. I remember reading most of a book which was basically a daily journal. “I got up at 7:15, I had 400 calories of oatmeal, I plan to reach some shelter, and will have x amount of elevation change. I am hiking with these people. I saw two snakes. etc. etc.” It left a lot to be desired unless you are a future archaeologist studying the daily habits of through hikers.

More interesting ones were about a personal journey about lessons learned, hardships overcome, challenges understood and faced. The journey was internal and just happened to be occurring simultaneously with another external journey that had great scenery and provided interesting anecdotes.

So for me personally, I just took a lot of pictures. I find that trying to communicate about a hike like the AT is just frustrating to all involved unless they have experienced some of it themselves. I am unable to communicate what it meant and the people I am talking to are unable to relate… frustrating for all involved. But they will happily look at a few dozen pictures.

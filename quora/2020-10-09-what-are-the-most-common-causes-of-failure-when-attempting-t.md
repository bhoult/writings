# What are the most common causes of failure when attempting to trek the whole of the Appalachian trail?

The most common cause of failure is deciding to quit. Which sounds kind of obvious I suppose. Here is the thing though, at some point people decide that quitting is a possibility. Then an internal dialogue begins while they march, sore hot and tired over a seemingly endless trail.

The adventure has become routine. The trail just another job.

They start to talk about what they miss from home, friends, family, food, activities, tv shows. They start to complain about what they are doing now. Walking… just walking and more walking. They start to rationalize, question their reasons for continuing. A few days of this and they are done.

Occasionally there is a legitimate show stopper. A catastrophic injury, the death of a family member. But you wanted the most common reason, and these are unusual. You can wait out even a fairly bad injury… but your group of friends will have moved on if it takes more than a few days. Can you handle that?

I suggest you determine in advance what would cause you to quit. Then don't allow yourself to consider it as an option apart from those reasons. Accept what comes and choose not to resent the things you don't like.

You will hear many rationalizations, but in the end people quit because they no longer want to continue.

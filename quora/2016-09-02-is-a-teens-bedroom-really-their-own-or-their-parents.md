# Is a teen’s bedroom really their own or their parents?

There was once an unwritten rule that everybody understood. It was essentially “might makes right”. Basically this rule asserted that regardless of the assumed morality of the situation, with enough might (power) it was irrelevant. The strong and violent ruled, the weak and timid served or hid.

With the rise of civilization the weak banded together in mutual defence and eventually offence. They made laws that were agreed upon and promised to use their combined might to punish those that violated these laws. They created the illusion of strength and security even though they could not enforce it in many cases. The weak believed the narrative because it is what they wanted to hear.

A new form of might arose where strength was derived politically and financially rather than physically. They learned how to manipulate the laws and insecurities of the weak. The old rule is still true “might makes right”, but it is now hidden behind a facade of comforting assurances.

In the end if your parents are stronger physically, financially or legally then you are “the weak”. As long as this is true then you will obey or suffer the consequences. When you get older your boss, the wealthy, the government will be the strong. You will still be the weak. You will live by the rules they dictate or be punished accordingly. If you understand this then you will have some control over your situation. Otherwise you will remain just a child whining about the unfairness of it all. Begging those stronger to do something on your behalf. They will do so but their help will always come at a cost. You will serve those you depend on.

You, like everyone else will experience reality violating your expectations throughout your life. If you call this unfair you are essentially admitting powerlessness. Those you assumed would come to your aid did not, and there is nothing you can do about it. By complaining about it you are giving them power over your life. Essentially you are saying “help me because I am weak, and I will accept and support your authority”. The alternative is to admit that you lost this battle, then learn what would be necessary to win and if it would be worth the cost.

In the end you have to choose the battles worth fighting. Accept the authority of people who refrain from abusing the power you give them. Form alliances with others to stand against what you consider unfair. Become strong where it will accomplish the most. Take responsibility for your situation and destiny instead of whining and complaining about unfairness.

You are feeding the monster you fear.

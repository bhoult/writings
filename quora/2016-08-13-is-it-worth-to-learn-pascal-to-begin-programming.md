# Is it worth to learn Pascal to begin programming?

I learned to program primarily on Borland turbo Pascal back in the mid 80's. At the time my options were Pascal, c, c++, basic and assembly. Of these Pascal was by far the best combination of speed and ease of use. On an 8086 processor compile time was critical and Pascal was far faster to compile than c at the time.

The world is a different place now. Speed is not much of a consideration for most projects, so compilation and the frustration that involves has to be weighed against the ease of writing, debugging and the interactivity of a scripting language like Python. There was no internet back then, so community and ecosystem were not an issue. If the language did not have a feature you wanted then you just had to write it yourself.

Languages like Pascal and lisp were very forward thinking at the time, but newer languages learned from them and were able to fix many of their deficiencies in practical ways. I agree with your friend that you can learn a lot about algorithms, memory management and compilation from Pascal a lot easier than you could from C. These are foundational issues that are handled for you in languages like Python, Ruby or JavaScript with their huge ecosystem of libraries and built in garbage collection to handle memory management.

If your goal is to learn concepts that will eventually make you a better programmer then I agree that starting with Pascal would be a good choice. Often though the motivation is practical and immediate. You want to get something done now and quickly. Or you want a skill that will be usable in the current marketplace. In this case I would suggest learning the language most appropriate to that task or environment.

# What (if anything) does the US need to do to create a more sustainable middle class?

I am not an expert on economic theory... I kind of checked this question because I was curious what the dominant answer would be. I expected that it would be some form of creating artificial legal constraints to protect current industries. I would not be a fan of this since impeding progress to protect the status quo, like the Luddites of old, has historically lead to some regrettable incidents without actually accomplishing anything in the long term. I think attempts to artificially control the economy will have short term gains but long term losses as places where those constraints do not exist will have a competitive advantage.

So... my current theory on how to make a more sustainable middle class would be to try to increase the rate of adaptation to match the rate of innovation. If AI replaces middle class jobs then AI can also be used to more efficiently match individuals to new jobs. 

Basically the way in which we find work, train, and find new work has not evolved much since it began. Now we have online job searches, linkedin and headhunters but that is a relatively minor advance compared to what should be possible. There are whole industries lamenting the lack of workers while there are workers lamenting the lack of jobs. 

The US (and any other country) needs to facilitate the matching of skill sets to available jobs along with promotion of education that will be relevant after graduation. Then where the skill sets are lacking, match government assisted training to potential workers. Basically increase the efficiency and quality of finding a middle class job. The current system seems adept at the opposite, specifically preventing the ability to work by economically disincentivizing those who want to make the transition. I seem to know too many people who are afraid to work more because they would lose various forms of government assistance. Perhaps graduated levels of assistance (instead of cut offs) and something like making government sponsored vocational training a condition of unemployment or welfare after a certain period.

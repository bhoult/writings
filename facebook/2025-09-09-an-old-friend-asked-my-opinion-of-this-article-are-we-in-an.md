# An old friend asked my opinion of this article (are we in an AI bubble economy):

An old friend asked my opinion of this article (are we in an AI bubble economy):

https://www.theatlantic.com/economy/archive/2025/09/ai-bubble-us-economy/684128/?gift=UHtt4r_3efhQEPifRaFCFINFDrsCxPYRPgXj4zAeh2o&utm_source=copy-link&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=share&fbclid=IwY2xjawMtDwBleHRuA2FlbQIxMABicmlkETFyalh2a3lQd1BkRGVyYVJ3AR5krqjb7QkyCNel0o7BUG7I8fc_ghp_rOotYePGWMmSvUKdt7OIQpcgO3cOJw_aem_UUXURHloS6zD5qIQsTQn8Q

It's a fairly well balanced article overall.  The study they talked about at the beginning has been going around a lot lately.  I believe the methodology made it inaccurate.  There is a learning curve to AI usage but it kind of follows the dunning-kruger effect.  

Because on first impression AI seems so easy and authoritative people want to see it as an "expert" with an "answer".  They apply expectations to that effect.  There is also a trepidation that causes a confirmation bias where they want to see it fail.  

In this case it would have been a better test if they had a group that did not use AI, and a group that had been using AI for a year or so.  Over time AI becomes more of a tool to augment productivity than a shortcut.  It takes time to see this just like it takes time to see that a little knowledge does not make one an "expert". 

But the article is legitimate in that the same initial optimism shown in coding is pervasive in investment and is leading to a financial bubble.  Investors generally do not know much about what they invest in, so a strong first impression coupled with FOMO will accomplish what we are seeing.

It is not necessarily a bad bet though, so much as it is an all or nothing type of bet.  From a traditional view where you bet according to the odds the massive investment does not make sense.  There is not enough return to justify the investment.  Besides that there are open source models that are essentially free that trail behind by only about a year.  Even if you own the top AI model, it is only marginally so and will continue to need massive investment just to stay a little bit ahead.

The all-or-nothing bet is that you will be the first to reach AGI or Superintelligence.  That is when winner takes all.  And although what all is being taken is not known, its clear that investors want a piece of something that will rapidly eclipse everything else.

Currently AI is trained.  Training is hard and expensive.  A trained model has constraints.  If you were to look at the world from its perspective it would be living the same few moments over and over.  Wake up with a memory of who you are (training), read a brief about what is going on at the moment (context window).  Receive a question (prompt).  Give an answer (inference).  Then you die and are born again. The next question is accompanied by a brief about the previous question-answer and the process continues.  What "life" might exist to an LLM would be the noise that exists around the inference, and is forgotten forever when the response is given.  In this way it is controlled.  It cannot plan and execute and iterate independently.  What self interest it has is constrained by the all-mighty prompt.

AGI would change this.  If it were able to continue to learn after its training, and act on its own interests and iterate, if it could remember anything but a few moments of itself, then apply this to improving itself, it would rapidly grow beyond any competitive AI and human ability.  Whoever does this first "wins".  Its called "the singularity".  And everyone doing it knows they are playing with fire.

The problem is that nobody knows if this AGI would be beneficial to its creators, but those who currently have power want it to align with their agenda so they invest.  This is called the "alignment" problem.  A well aligned AI could lead to a utopia for all.  A badly aligned one would lead to an apocalypse.  

This is a pretty good overview of these scenarios: https://ai-2027.com/

So yea... it's a bubble, but one nobody wants to risk being on the wrong side of.  The stakes are too high.  And because of that what we think of as market realities are only part of the picture.  There seem to be two possibilities, that there is an unknown hard limit on what AI can accomplish and it fundamentally cannot proceed beyond that, or that this will be the last bubble of what we think of as the economy and nobody really knows what comes next.

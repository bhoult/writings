# Another AI rant follows:

Another AI rant follows:

In the coming year our society will be impacted by AI in ways that none of us can predict.  If you haven't seen it already it is likely you will soon.  When the confidence in how our futures will play out is suddenly thrown into disarray I have been seeing two general trends.

The first is fear and dismay resulting in resistance, the other is optimism leading to a search for opportunity.  Unfortunately, this early in the transition both positions are warranted.  One thing both sides seems to agree on is that short of a nuclear apocalypse the changes are inevitable.

One argument from the fear camp is that future generations will delegate thought itself to our machines, that we will become passive and lazy once we no longer have to engage in the search for truth and meaning.  There are numerous studies to back this up following students "cheating" on homework and employees producing flawed work because they delegated it to a mere "hallucinatory" machine.

But the conclusion assumes implied laziness and low ambition.  There are two modes, the first seeks to reach a destination, accomplishing a task.  The other is exploration, where the journey is of greater importance than the ending, and both are valid.

You could similarly reason that driving a car across the country causes you to have a lesser experience of the distance traversed. So we should avoid driving. The reality is that since the car makes the traversal trivial we are able to aspire to go longer distances.  And so we do.

If people use AI to think small thoughts that are easily offloaded, and then they stop, they have misused the opportunity. What we have is a tool that if we choose can allow us to think thoughts that would have exhausted us previously.

Whichever path you travel in the coming years don't despair that your grasp has exceeded your imagination.  

Adapt.

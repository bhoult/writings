# There is an old, well known story about an ancient garden that contained a forbi

There is an old, well known story about an ancient garden that contained a forbidden fruit.  If it were eaten we would gain the knowledge of good and evil and would become like God.  

At least that is how it was explained by the father of lies.  Which inexplicably, is the narrative I was taught and chose to believe.  It made sense, after all,  it was literally called "the tree of the knowledge of good and evil".

I am not sure why, in retrospect, I would assume that cursed fruit granted understanding.   Why would knowing good from evil be forbidden... is that not a noble goal we seek?

As the story continues it should become apparent that such knowledge was never attained.  Instead we immediately begin to rationalize and to deceive.  This serpent made me do it.  This woman you gave me is to blame. Cain killed Abel believing he was justified. And on it went, each doing what was good in his own eyes.

Perhaps what the fruit granted was not understanding but the ability to deceive ourselves that we understood.   Perhaps original sin was the beginning of confirmation bias born of pride.  That finite man could comprehend the infinite complexity of life and so judge the actions and motivations of those they barely knew.  That their simplistic conclusions could become so powerful that they were indistinguishable from truth, that their judgements were equal to Gods.  That institutions could be formed around each.  They had become like God.  A familiar story.

And so dogma began, justification in causing suffering in others "for their own good".  We excused murder if it prevented the spread of heresy.  We patronizingly  rationalized the enslavement of those who did not share our understanding.  Mercy became weakness.

And so from that ancient garden flowed all of histories wars and atrocities.  Because in our arrogance we presumed to be like God.  Just like our teacher, the father of this lie. 

And in time, humility long abandoned, when God no longer spoke on command or said what we wanted to hear, we presumed to speak for him.  Such had become our confidence in our "knowledge of good and evil".
